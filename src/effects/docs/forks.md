# Forks

Rules to respect when changing or diagnosing interventions, damage-step probes, protection forks or directed forks, and when registering a directed rule.

- What is specified: `specs/2026-10-30-ability-effect-model-gen3.md` § 6 (directed forks) and § 8.
- Code: `forge-connector/src/main/java/com/pricepredictor/connector/effects/` (`Forks`, `ForkFidelity`, `ForkBudget`, `InterventionScheduler`, `InterventionFork`, `ForcedResolution`, `DamageStepProbe`, `ProtectionFork`, `ForkPlayForward`) and its `directed/` package.
- Flag signatures of fork records: `record-format.md`.

## Rules for every fork

- Every fork is score-checked against the live game before any perturbation. A copy that already disagrees is measuring the copier.
- Both games are scored from one seeded random stream of the check's own (`Forks.score`). Before the second main phase the evaluator has the AI declare the coming combat in a copy, drawing from Forge's global `MyRandom`, so an exact copy scored from that stream simulated a different combat.
- The score weighs only part of the board, so every copy is also audited against the live game's snapshot by entity and field, lists compared as multisets. Differences are printed as `fork fidelity:` lines. The audit is a report: it keeps the fork. What the copier left out is also stamped on every record of the fork as `copy_dropped`, which decides nothing either.
- A discarded fork still counts against its budget. Otherwise a systematically failing copy retries until the game ends.
- The copy holds no sideboard. A static effect touching nothing the copy holds is not undone in it (`GameCopier.outsideTheCopy`): a characteristic-defining ability applies in every zone and Forge's static check visits the sideboard, so a sealed pool card left out of the deck (Serpent of the Endless Sea, a changeling) has an effect on itself there, and undoing it failed the copy. An effect the copy does hold is undone over the cards the copy holds and leaves the others out (`GameCopier.UndoMap`): a copied effect card's static can list sideboard cards (Wish, Sovereign's Realm, Frontier Explorer: play a card from outside the game), and mapping them failed the copy. A copy's token keeps its original's paper card, which copying it as a permanent builds from.
- A fork must leave the live game untouched. Every fork reads the live game's counters, random stream identity, players and stack before and after (`Forks.LiveMarks`); a difference is counted as `fork <kind> moved the live game`. `ForkIsolationTest` plays seeded matches with forks off and with all four kinds on and compares the live records byte for byte; two of its match sets take 704.5f forks whose forced cast a plain AI seat (a `ForcingSeatController`) and a random seat (`ForcedPlaySeat`) make.
- A fork draws from a random stream of its own (`Forks.isolated`), never from Forge's global `MyRandom`, which the live game draws from.
- A copy, a score check, a fork body or a fork's writer that throws is a failure, counted under `fork <kind> copy`, `score check`, `body` or `write` in the worker's hook-failure line.
- A fork that declines for an expected reason throws a `ForkRefusal`, counted by kind and reason and printed on a line of its own. A refusal is never a failure and counts toward no failure threshold or exit code.
- No fork resolves a restart of the game (FR-010d): Forge's `RestartGameEffect` resets the JVM-wide trigger id counter, and the live game's later triggers would take ids its own triggers hold. `Forks.refuseARestart` ends the fork as the refusal `game restart` before each main-loop step a fork played on takes, which is the check that acts: a restart put on the stack resolves at the earliest in the step after. The checks once the copy is made and before each item a forced resolution resolves (`ForcedResolution`) are belts no game reaches: `GameCopier` copies no stack (`GameSimulator.COPY_STACK` is off, and even on it copies spells only, while Karn Liberated's restart is a loyalty ability), and a forced resolution's stack holds only its forced line, never a restart (`InterventionScheduler`), and what that line's cast triggered. `LiveMarks` stays the backstop.
- No fork of any kind is taken at a phase event published while the stack resolves (`GameRecording.forksAt`): an effect ending the turn or the combat phase publishes the steps it moves to from inside its resolution.
- At most two forks may target one real resolution. This is a fixed constant no flag can raise.
- The score check fails at the beginning-of-combat phase event, because the evaluator reads a combat Forge has not built yet. No scheduler takes a fork there (`Forks.scoreCheckable`).
- The real-versus-fork difference is computed at evaluation time and never becomes a training target.

## Interventional resolutions

- An intervention forks the game and forces an ability the AI never casts. It writes the effect half alone: there was no real activation, so there is no cost half to link to.
- The ability goes on the fork's stack directly, not through `ComputerUtil.handlePlayingSpellAbility`. The cost is usually the reason the record is missing, and routing through cost payment succeeds only on free plays; a first attempt that way produced nothing but land drops.
- A line is forced at a step's first priority with the stack empty (`InterventionScheduler.prioritySink`, on the priority-settled hook, armed by the step's phase event), never at the phase event itself. Forge publishes the phase event before the step's turn-based action (the draw, the attack declaration, the combat damage) and also for a step the turn then skips, so a line forced there resolves on a board no player gets priority on: a draw step before the draw, a combat-damage step on a turn with no attack. Priority settles after the state-based check and before the player holding it chooses, never inside the AI's own evaluation of its candidates, which would copy a game mid-decision. The protection fork stays at the phase event.
- The budget (`--interventions-per-game`) is spread over the game's turns, not spent where the game starts (`InterventionScheduler.spreadOverTheGame`, drawn from the game's seed alone against `TYPICAL_GAME_TURNS` = 20, a constant with no flag). A game shorter than typical may end with budget unspent.
- A line is forced only where its own restrictions let its holder play it but for its cost (`InterventionScheduler.playableNow`): every check of its playability verdict but `cost` passes (`PlayabilityVerdict`): its timing, `ActivationPhases$` and `PlayerTurn$` (a forecast only in its holder's upkeep), `IsPresent$` and the script's other conditions, its activation limits, its zone and a static forbidding it. A spell whose mana cost is no cost (Ancestral Vision) is never forced, nor anything at untap or cleanup. A special action (`AbilityStatic`: suspend, foretell, plot) is never forced.
- Among the lines a boundary can force, the one forced is drawn at random from the fork's stream (`InterventionScheduler.Forcing.shuffle`, the `InterventionFork`'s seeded `Random`), never the first in hand order: that order spends the budget on the creature spells the AI casts anyway.
- A line without the modes or targets its resolution needs on the live board spends no fork and is not marked forced, so a removal spell held over an empty board is forced once a target arrives. A charm's modes are drawn only among the modes whose targets exist.
- Each line is forced once a game per pass. A line is its printed line's provenance key, without its holder, so a mirror whose players hold the same lines forces each once and may leave budget unspent.
- A line whose text branches on whose turn it is (`ConditionPlayerTurn$`, `ConditionOpponentTurn$`) is forced twice a game at most: once on its holder's own turn and once at a boundary of another player's turn, where it shows its other branch. `InterventionScheduler` offers those first at each boundary that draws a line.
- A line restarting the game (Karn Liberated's) is never forced: Forge's restart resets the JVM-wide trigger id counter.
- A line whose resolution reads a cost the fork never paid where Forge reads it unguarded is never forced (`InterventionFork.readsAnUnpaidCost`): ninjutsu and a sneak cast read the attacker returned to hand, and forced, each throws in the engine. `InterventionScheduler` passes such a line over as it does a game restart, so it takes neither the boundary nor one of the game's planned interventions, and counts it once a game as the refusal `intervention reads a cost it never paid`. A crew or saddle is forced: its forced paid list is null, which Forge adds to the vehicle's or mount's list for the turn as nothing, the first time in a turn or a second.
- A forced resolution is recorded by the live bracket's own code (`ForcedResolution`): a fork-bound `GameRecording`, as `ForkPlayForward` records a fork it plays on, to which the live game's hooks hand the fork's callbacks. A second set of filing rules for forced halves drifts from the live ones, and a forced half then describes an outcome differently from a live half of the same play.
- Of everything the fork's recording writes, only the forced item's effect halves are kept: the halves under its cost half's link, one per chosen mode of a modal item, fizzled or not. Each is written without the link, since the cost half describes a payment the fork never made and is not written, and marked `interventional` and `fork`. What the cast triggered and what the resolution caused have records of their own live, and none here.
- The fork's stack is resolved only until the forced item has left it, then the state-based check after it and the triggers it left: nothing after that is the forced item's. Closing the fork's recording writes the half the next action would have closed.
- `ForcedAndLiveHalvesTest` checks that a play resolved live and forced on the same board gives effect halves that agree member for member apart from the fork members, among them an enchanted creature killed in the state-based check, a fizzled charm, an effect ending the turn, a self-copying spell and an unless cost paid with mana.
- A forced resolution that began to resolve is written whatever it did, as a live one is. One that never began to resolve writes nothing and is the refusal `intervention never resolved`.

## Damage-step probes

- A probe re-runs one combat with a keyword stripped. It forks at the phase event, which fires before the turn-based action that deals the damage: the only moment the copy can diverge from a board where nothing has been dealt yet.
- The damage step is the probe's whole fork, and `ForkEventSink` files every event of it, the clause and outcome hooks' events handed to it through `Forks.ForkHooks`. The sink records probes only; a forced resolution is recorded by the live bracket's code (§ Interventional resolutions).
- The keyword comes off through the layer system, not the printed list, so a printed, equipped and until-end-of-turn keyword all go the way the rules would take them.
- The branch is held rather than written. It mirrors the real combat record, and that record has no id until the step the probe forked ahead of has finished; `BusBracketCollector.onCombatRecord` hands the id over. Reconstructing the pairing from board state would break on two identical-looking combats in one turn.
- A probe's payload names both the keyword and the carrier. The keyword alone is ambiguous on a board with two tramplers, and an evaluator reproducing the perturbation model-side would strip the wrong creature.
- A creature is probed once per keyword however many spellings it carries (trample and trample over planeswalkers), every spelling stripped. First strike is never probed at the regular step, where it deals nothing with or without it. A step's stale branches are dropped before anything else.
- `--probe-keywords` is the runtime switch, deliberately separate from the build decision gate 2 drives.

## Protection forks

- As a first main phase begins, a protection fork copies the game, gives one creature a rare protection through the `keyword` operation and plays the copy to the end of the turn through `ForkPlayForward`.
- The quality is one that an opposing non-land permanent, an opposing hand card or a card attached to the creature has, spelled as `rule_outcomes.ProtectionQuality.fragment` spells it.
- Its records are of any kind, cost halves linked to their effect halves included.
- It has a budget of its own: one a game, drawn at each first main phase with a creature out. The forced resolutions spend theirs before any creature is out.

## Directed forks

- A directed fork is played on, not stopped. `ForkPlayForward` plays from the phase the copy was taken in until its cleanup step leaves nothing to resolve or the game ends, with both seats choosing afresh.
- A variant may keep its bound records (FR-010c, `KeptRecords`, named by `Application.keepingCandidatesOf` / `keepingNegativesOf` from the fork's binding). In its fork the named card's decision candidates are written at every priority stop without drawing either sampling stage, and the not-fired evaluations of the triggers it hosts are offered whatever `TriggerNegativeSampler`'s balance says and count toward no mode's balance. Deduplication and the per-line cap of 24 not-fired records still apply. Without it, a rule whose point is a sampled candidate or a trigger negative comes back with nothing its detector can tag: a fork's recording is fresh, so the sampler holds no firing to repay.
- Kept records are ordinary fork records: they count toward `--max-records-per-game`, enter no reservoir, and draw nothing from the live game's sampling stream. Cards are named by id, which a card keeps through the fork's zone changes; a card the edit puts in another's place has a new id.
- A variant may force one play (FR-010b, `ForcedPlay` on its `Application`, run by `ForcedPlayRun`): a cast or activation of a bound source's line in the fork's own step, an answer to a "you may" or a "you may pay" a named line of a bound source asks (by default its triggered abilities, `Line.TRIGGER`), or a bound creature's attack in the turn's declare-attackers step. Its roles are read in the fork's binding after the edit, so a play the edit makes possible (a land added for its mana) is found; a card the edit replaced is gone, so a target on it is left drawn. A card the edit makes (an `add_permanent`'s Elfhame Sanctuary, whose question an answer names) has no live counterpart: `Application.making` binds it on the edited copy (`afterEdit`, read by `DirectedFork` and the registry twin) and lists its role in `Application.makes`. The dry run on the live board skips only what the play names of such a card (the line when it is the source, a targeting clause, a cost card, the defender) and checks the rest; skipping the whole play would leave every other refusal to the fork, after its budget is spent. A play whose source is such a card is skipped whole: without the line, nothing of it can be checked on the live board.
- A modal line (a charm) is no line a forced play makes, and its forced play has no legal line: `RandomSpellPlayer.play` asks a charm's modes afresh (`CharmEffect.makeChoices`), which would undo the modes and named targets the play drew.
- An answer is given only to a question its own line asks, never to another line of the same source. A forced yes to a "you may pay" pays the cost whenever `ComputerUtilCost.canPayCost` says it can be paid (`ForcedPlayRun.pay`), through `CostPayment.payComputerCosts`, never through the AI's `payCostToPreventEffect`, which first asks the AI's `willPayUnlessCost` and would pay only what the AI would have paid anyway. Both seats route the question there.
- A forced play's state is read and written on the game's thread only. Forge's AI evaluates its candidates on a thread of its own ("Game AI Eval", which may outlive a timeout), but that thread only evaluates: it resolves, pays and confirms nothing in the game it evaluates, and a full simulation plays in a copy whose players have controllers of their own.
- A forced play is made through the engine's own paid cast path (`RandomSpellPlayer.play`), its cost decided by `ForcedPlayRun.RoleCostDecision` and its mana paid by the AI, so the fork's recorders write it as a live play. Never through `InterventionFork`'s forced resolution: that pushes the ability onto the stack unpaid, and the fork's cost record would describe a payment that never happened.
- The forcing seat exists in the copy only. A random seat takes the play itself (`ForcedPlaySeat`); a plain AI seat is replaced by a `ForcingSeatController` built from the same lobby player with the same simulation mode. Every decision the play does not name stays the seat's.
- A forced play that cannot be made is a counted refusal, never a failure, and writes nothing: `forced play has no legal line` (found on the live board by `ForcedPlayRun.dryRun` before the fork is taken, which spends no budget and leaves the rule its one fork of the game, else on the edited copy), `forced play not made` (its step passed, or the turn ended), `forced play cost not paid` (the AI's own check says the line cannot be paid at the first priority where the rules let it be played, before the card moves; its payment or a named cost card failed; a "you may pay" answered yes cannot be paid). The controller only flags; `ForkPlayForward` throws between main-loop steps and after the turn, before `writeForkLines`: a refusal flagged in the turn's last step, or an answer whose question is never asked, is thrown only after the turn.
- A forced play draws its unnamed targets and X from a stream of its own, seeded from the next of the game's sequence of fork seeds (`ForkPlayForward`), which only a fork that forces draws. Every later fork of the game takes the next seeds of that sequence, so a forcing fork shifts the seeds of the forks after it; the games stay reproducible from their seed.
- No record member marks a forced play: a directed fork's records may hold a play its seat was made to make, and which variants force what is fixed per rule in the registry. Python treats directed records as it treats every aimed record (no rule-test record, `aimed` in `rule-counts`, natural in the rule mask).
- On stopping, the fork's bracket writes what no later event would close: the cleanup's turn-based actions and the held half, or everything the game's end writes when the fork ended the game. Forge publishes the game's end only from a match.
- It is recorded by a fork-bound `GameRecording` that installs no hook. The live game's `PatchedCollectors` hand every hook callback to the fork's collectors too, and each channel keeps its own game's. Dispatch is by game identity, because the AI evaluates on a thread of its own.
- The fork's lines are held and written only once it has played out, so a fork that throws leaves nothing.
- Its random seat draws from the game's fork seed. Nothing in a fork draws from the match's source, which would shift later games' seats.
- The copy carries the turn's pending state (delayed triggers, end-of-turn and leaves-the-battlefield commands, controller timestamps) through the `GameCopier` patch. A command whose closure holds a reference the copy cannot map is left out rather than carried with a live reference into the real game.
- The edit is applied before the fork's collectors start listening: the edits are silent to the rules but still publish bus events.
- The budget, the rule's one fork per game and the lifetime tally are all spent when the fork is taken, before the score check.
- A directed fork's records carry no `mirror_of`: a phase boundary closes every live record first.
- Every edit ends with the static check and a fresh last-known battlefield (`BoardEdit.settle`).
- An edit can refuse: `replace_card` on a permanent it cannot carry over faithfully, a `keyword` edit the card cannot have, a removal of a keyword it does not carry. A refused edit spends the fork and is counted in the refusal tally, never as a failure.
- A fork played on stops in a cleanup step only when the engine will not take it again (514.3a).
- Untap-step `turn_action` records and directed forks are taken at the untap boundary as anywhere.

## Registering a directed rule

- A rule is registered in `FE/directed/DirectedRules.java` as a `DirectedRule`, and each rule is a class of its own in `FE/directed/rules/`, its precondition a hand-written read of the live board at a named phase boundary.
- `DirectedRulesContractTest` checks every registration against `rules/scenarios/*.json`. `RegisteredRulesTest` builds each scenario's base on a `TestBoard`: the precondition must bind there and not on a board missing the base, and the edited copy must differ from the live board by the variant's one fact.
- The registered variant makes the side the rule's counts lack. Where that side needs a rare fact, the registered scenario's base is the common board and its variant adds the fact (Felidar Sovereign's controller below 40 life, set to 40); a variant taking the fact away records what live games already show. The side a fork makes is what the Python detectors tag its records, not the variant's polarity label: a variant labelled `other` whose forced play makes the lacking side is registered (205.1a, 111.10p).
- A detector declaration reads the bit the variant changes: a cost shortfall is `affordable`, never `can_play`, which Forge computes without the cost and which fails at every opponent's priority stop on timing alone.
- A rule no single board edit yields the lacking side of leaves the list with a reason of its own in `NOT_DIRECTED` (`scripts/rules_catalogue.py`); its scenarios and detectors stay.
- A precondition needing a card no edition with a Draft booster prints (`BoosterTemplates.eligibleSealedSets`) binds only in a steered `collect-variants` round, never in `match-outcomes` sealed play: 111.10p (Ellivere of the Wild Court), 701.44d (Hakbal of the Surging Soul) and 701.50f (a "whenever a creature connives" card) stay listed for that round (spec FR-014b).
- A precondition only finds the board where the rule can happen. It cannot force the live game's decisions, so a precondition on a spell in hand holds only as long as the AI has not yet cast it; a variant that needs the play its point follows forces it in the fork (FR-010b) and binds the actor, the source and every target and cost card as roles.
- A forced line is named by a string (`ForcedPlay.Line.api("ExchangeLifeVariant")`, `Line.keyword("TRANSMUTE")`) or a predicate read only when a fork asks, never a constant of a Forge enum: the registry is built where Forge is not initialised.
- A precondition binds only boards where the variant's edit stays true for the fork's turn: a sole permanent for a removal, no land left to play for a "one land short" variant, a spell castable at this boundary, a replaced permanent whose replacing card survives what the edit carries. The registry contract is `specs/025-ability-effect-model-gen3/contracts/catalogue-extensions.md` § Directed-rule registry.
- `RegisteredRulesTest`'s twin also runs a forcing variant's dry run on the live board and checks that its forced line is playable on the edited copy. It also puts each rule's edited copy through the state-based check its first priority makes, which must leave the board, every life total and every player as they were. `DirectedForkPlayedForwardTest` plays one rule per operation family forward, every kept-record rule with no candidate sampled, every inverted rule and every forcing rule, and finds the variant's point in the fork's records. With `-Ddirected.records.dump=DIR` it copies each case's records to `DIR`, for tagging with the Python detectors (`tag_record`): play every rule you bind or rebind forward once and check both sides are tagged as its scenario says.
- The supervisor's startup check (`directed_fork_error` in `sealed/infrastructure/effect_collection_flags.py`) requires, for each listed rule, a declared detector that is not `none`, not `same_as` and not untaggable, a contrasting variant classified into an operation, and a registration.
- `sealed` may not import `effects`, so that check reads `rules/scenarios/` itself. `tests/unit/sealed/infrastructure/test_directed_fork_flags.py` pins it against `rules_catalogue_io` over the real catalogue and runs it over the whole real `rules/directed-fork-rules.txt`, which must pass with no rule refused.
