# Forks

Covers interventional resolutions, damage-step probes, protection forks and directed forks. Read on demand: `src/effects/CLAUDE.md` says when, and lists the other topic files in this directory.

## Interventions and damage-step probes

Some abilities are never cast in any game Forge's AI plays — it judges them bad, cannot afford them, or never draws them into a board where they do anything — so no amount of observation produces a record. An **interventional resolution** forks the game and forces one, writing the effect half alone (there was no real activation, so there is no cost half to link to). A **damage-step probe** re-runs one combat with a keyword stripped; it carries `fork = true` and `interventional = false`, which distinguishes it from an intervention by flags alone, plus `mirror_of`.

Two choices in `intervene` are what make it produce anything. The ability goes **on the fork's stack directly** rather than through `ComputerUtil.handlePlayingSpellAbility`, because the cost is usually the reason the record is missing: routing through cost payment succeeds only on free plays, which is how a first attempt produced nothing but land drops. And the candidate is chosen at a **phase boundary**, never inside the AI's own evaluation of its candidates: forking there copies a game mid-decision and re-enters the rules engine underneath it.

A probe forks at the **phase event**, which fires before the turn-based action that deals the damage — the only moment the copy can diverge from a board where nothing has been dealt yet. The keyword comes off through the layer system rather than the printed list, so a printed, equipped and until-end-of-turn keyword all go the way the rules would take them.

The branch is **held rather than written**, because it mirrors the real combat record and that record has no id until the step the probe forked ahead of has finished; `BusBracketCollector.onCombatRecord` hands the id over when it writes one. Reconstructing the pairing from board state instead would break on two identical-looking combats in one turn.

A probe's payload names **both the keyword and the carrier** (`probed_keyword`, `probed_entity`). The keyword alone is ambiguous on a board with two tramplers, and an evaluator reproducing the perturbation model-side would strip the wrong creature — comparing against a counterfactual nobody ran. Both fields are absent on an observed combat record, which perturbs nothing.

Three rules the budgets depend on: every fork is score-checked against the live game **before** any perturbation (a copy that already disagrees is measuring the copier — both games are scored from one seeded random stream of the check's own, `Forks.score`, because before the second main phase the evaluator has the AI declare the coming combat in a copy, drawing from Forge's global `MyRandom`, and an exact copy scored from that stream simulated a different combat; a disagreement prints `score mismatch detail` with the evaluator's breakdown lines one game has and the other lacks, `ScoreMismatchReport`; the rating weighs only part of the board, so every copy is also audited against the live game's board snapshot, global block, players and entities by id and field, links as a multiset, every list of keywords or ability lines (`granted_temporary.keywords`/`abilities`, `lines` and their `keys`, `emblems`, `source_lines`) as a multiset too — order never matters, a repeated stacking keyword does — and its differences printed as `fork fidelity:` lines naming entity and field — a report that keeps the fork, `ForkFidelity`); a discarded fork **still counts against its budget** (otherwise a systematically failing copy retries until the game ends); and at most **two** forks may target one real resolution — a fixed constant no flag can raise. `--probe-keywords` is the runtime switch and is deliberately separate from the build decision gate 2 drives; it names every damage-step keyword unless a run narrows or empties it.

## Lines that branch on whose turn it is

A line whose text branches on whose turn it is (a clause carrying `ConditionPlayerTurn$` or `ConditionOpponentTurn$`, `FE/TurnConditions.java`) is forced twice a game at most: once on its holder's own turn, as every line is, and once at a boundary of another player's turn, for its holder (FR-057), where it shows its other branch. `InterventionScheduler` offers those first at each boundary, because the forced resolutions otherwise spend the whole per-game budget at the game's first boundary, under the same budgets and the per-resolution cap.

## Protection forks

A **protection fork** (FR-058, `FE/ProtectionFork.java`) is an intervention played on: as a first main phase begins it copies the game, gives one creature a rare protection through the `keyword` operation (a card type, a creature type, a condition or everything that an opposing non-land permanent, an opposing hand card or a card attached to the creature has, spelled as `rule_outcomes.ProtectionQuality.fragment` spells it, `FE/ProtectionQualities.java`) and plays the copy to the end of the turn through `ForkPlayForward`. Every record it writes carries `interventional = true` and `fork = true`, and is of any kind, cost halves linked to their effect halves included. One a game, on a run taking interventions, drawn with probability 0.25 at each first main phase with a creature out (`ForkBudget.MAX_PROTECTION_FORKS_PER_GAME`, `ProtectionFork.PROBABILITY`): a budget of its own, because the forced resolutions spend theirs before any creature is out.

## Directed forks

A **directed fork** is played on rather than stopped: `ForkPlayForward` begins the phase the copy was taken in (the copy sits before that phase's turn-based actions, so `PhaseHandler.onPhaseBegin` is reached reflectively) and loops `mainLoopStep` until its cleanup step leaves nothing to resolve (the next step would begin the next turn's untap step) or the game ends, with both seats choosing afresh; on stopping, the fork's bracket writes what no later event would close: the cleanup's turn-based actions and the held half, or, when the fork ended the game, everything the game's end writes (the lethal combat record and the outcome), since Forge publishes the game's end only from a match. It is recorded by a fork-bound `GameRecording` that installs no hook: the live game's `PatchedCollectors` hand every hook callback to the fork's collectors too while it plays, and each channel keeps its own game's — dispatch by game identity, because the AI evaluates on a thread of its own. The fork's lines are held and written only once it has played out, so a fork that throws leaves nothing. Its random seat is attached with draws from the game's fork seed (`GamePlayer.attachForkSeats`); nothing in a fork draws from the match's source, which would shift later games' seats. The copy carries the turn's pending state — delayed triggers, end-of-turn and leaves-the-battlefield commands, controller timestamps — through the `GameCopier` patch on `effect-record-hooks`; a command whose closure holds a reference the copy cannot map is left out rather than carried with a live reference into the real game. One known gap: the score check fails at the beginning-of-combat phase event (the evaluator reads a combat Forge has not built yet), so a fork there is always discarded, and neither the directed nor the intervention scheduler takes one there (`Forks.scoreCheckable`). The intervention scheduler forces nothing at the untap boundary either, where nobody can act (`GameRecording.collectsTheBoard`); the untap step's turn_action records and directed forks (a stun counter, a skipped upkeep trigger) are taken there as anywhere.

A rule is registered in `FE/directed/DirectedRules.java` as a `DirectedRule`: its rule id, the scenario whose base variant it waits for (a rule can have several scenarios, and two can share a variant label), a `BoardPrecondition` returning the `Binding` of live objects the variants act on, and per variant label an `Application` naming its `ForkOperation` and building the `BoardEdit` from the fork's binding. `DirectedRulesContractTest` checks every registration against `rules/scenarios/*.json`. Each registered rule is a class of its own in `FE/directed/rules/`, its precondition a hand-written read of the live board at a named phase boundary (`Boards` holds the shared reads), and `RegisteredRulesTest` builds each scenario's base on a `TestBoard`: the precondition binds there and not on a board missing the base, and a `GameCopier` copy edited by the variant differs from the live board by the variant's one fact. A precondition only finds the board where the rule can happen; it cannot force the live game's or the fork's decisions, so a precondition on a spell in hand (704.5f's -N/-N) holds only as long as the AI has not yet cast it. `DirectedFork` copies and score-checks the game, maps the binding into the copy, applies the edit, and only then hands the copy to `ForkPlayForward`, whose collectors start listening after the edit: the edits are silent to the rules but still publish bus events. Every record of the fork carries `aimed_at = [rule]`, `fork = true`, `interventional = false`, `directed = {variant, operation}`, and `mirror_of` only when a live record was still open at the fork point (the held resolution's half), which a phase boundary has already closed.

The supervisor's startup check is `directed_fork_error` in `sealed/infrastructure/effect_collection_flags.py`, called by `match-outcomes` and `collect-variants` (exit 1 naming the rule): a listed rule needs a detector declared in its scenarios (not `none`), a contrasting variant classified into an operation, and a registration, which one run of `DirectedRulesMain` lists; a probability outside (0, 1] is refused too. `sealed` may not import `effects`, so the check reads `rules/scenarios/` itself (`rule_readiness`), and `tests/unit/sealed/infrastructure/test_directed_fork_flags.py` pins it against `rules_catalogue_io` over the real catalogue.
