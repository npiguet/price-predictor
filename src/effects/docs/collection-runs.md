# Collection runs

Covers `collect-coverage`, `collect-variants`, `validate-corpus` and `field-coverage`. Read on demand: `src/effects/CLAUDE.md` says when, and lists the other topic files in this directory.

- **`collect-coverage`** — sealed self-play only ever plays what sealed pools contain, so a card in no sealed-legal set never appears in a record at all. This builds decks over the **whole** converted corpus, weighted toward the cards with the fewest records, and plays rounds until every card is satisfied or retired. Each round plays `--decks-per-round` (default 500) of those decks as matches, counted from a per-run progress file under `--effect-records` — not itself a sealed corpus, and read by nothing downstream — which is what lets a round end at all rather than running until signalled. Three rules: the castability consult **only ranks** (never drops — being in a game is the precondition an interventional fork starts from, and a card the consult calls uncastable is exactly the one an intervention has to force into play); satisfaction counts acting hosts, event subjects and referenced refs but **not** mere snapshot presence (a card sitting on the battlefield while something else resolves teaches nothing about that card), and only in the gen-3 records `build-corpus` keeps (no `turn_action`, none its refusal rule refuses), a `continuous` record crediting its static's host, and every entity counted as the converted card its printed script file is (a sidecar's `card`), so "Fire // Ice" in hand, "Ice" on the stack or a back face credit `fire`; and a card with no new qualifying record for `--no-progress-rounds` rounds whose games showed it retires, which is the only thing that makes the run terminate. A round charges a stalled round only to the cards its games showed, because a worker draws its decks with replacement and some written decks are never played; a card written into decks but never shown retires after `UNSEEN_RETIRE_FACTOR` (3) times as many rounds. Shard counting runs on `--workers` processes, and each run's decks go to its own `coverage-decks.<run_id>.txt` (`variant-decks.<run_id>.txt` for `collect-variants`), since every worker reloads the file at its start. Both collectors exit 1 at startup without a holdout source — `--exclude-cards`, `--split-from` or `--training-corpus` (FR-058g); the held-out round, `--only-cards`, needs none — and when the connector JAR, `java` or a list file it reads is missing; a worker pool none of whose slots can start a worker ends the run with exit 1 (`ForgeWorkerPool` gives up after `MAX_FAILED_STARTS` failed starts in a row in every slot). `--training-corpus DIR` names the corpus this run extends and the `holdout-cards.txt` it was depleted against in one flag, refusing a directory without a list rather than running undepleted, and refusing either flag it implies beside it. Point it at the **depleted** corpus: discovery recurses, so a tree also holding the full-strength corpus would count validation records toward coverage and a card whose only records are held out would read as satisfied. `--exclude-cards` names the held-out cards to keep out of every deck, reading the list `holdout-cards` writes; it needs no trained model, so this command no longer waits on a training run that is supposed to follow it. `--split-from` is the alternative for adding coverage to a corpus an existing checkpoint trained on, and passing both is refused — two spellings of one holdout is a disagreement nothing would report. It reports **two residues** — cards judged uncastable, and castable cards short of `--target-records` — each retired card under its consult verdict; both fall to interventional resolutions. The coverage unit and the deck candidates come from `output/cardsfolder/` alone: a token script is not a deckable card and could never be satisfied or retired. Cards of a non-deck type (scheme, vanguard, plane, phenomenon, conspiracy, contraption, attraction, dungeon) leave both too, in the held-out round as well: Forge's card database does not load them as deck cards, so a deck holding one never materializes, and with no records they weigh the most and land in most decks. `--directed-fork-rules LIST` is a label only here: no directed fork runs and no startup check applies, and the rules go to every record's `aimed_at`.

- **`collect-variants`** — takes `--directed-fork-rules`, `--directed-forks-per-game` (2) and `--directed-fork-probability` (0.25) as `match-outcomes` does, startup check included (`forks.md` § Directed forks), the listed rules also aiming every record. Reads Forge's **source** scripts (not the converted ones), perturbs one parameter, and plays the result. A whitelisted numeric parameter shifts by up to ±3 or doubles, floored at one, never zero (a zero amount can make a free ability one that changes nothing, which the Forge AI re-activates without end: gen-2's Sage of Hours at `CounterNum$ 0`); a signed one (`NumAtt$ +2`, `NumDef$ -2`) keeps its sign and a magnitude of at least one, so a pump stays a pump and a debuff a debuff; deck-building hints (`DeckHints:`, `DeckNeeds:`, `DeckHas:`) and `AIPreference` SVars are never perturbed; a card-selecting parameter (`Valid…`, `…Valid`, `ChangeType`, `Affected` and a few more, `script_variants.selects_cards`) swaps its selector from a checked-in whitelist, never a duration, type name, chain label or description that reads like one (gen-2's `Duration$ Permanent` swapped to a selector ended an animation at once and a state trigger re-fired it, thousands of records a game); a protection keyword line (`K:Protection from black`) swaps its quality to a rare one from `PROTECTION_QUALITY_WHITELIST` — card types, conditions, everything and fifteen common creature types, spelled as `ProtectionQuality.fragment` spells them (FR-058). The whitelists exist because a shifted `CounterType` or a generated selector produces a script that fails to load rather than a card that plays differently. `--round` runs one of two rounds (root spec § 8.15; `domain/variant_rounds.py`). A steered deck holds one rule's variants and names the rule in a fourth deck-file column (`LABEL;SET_CODE;Cards;RULE`, `deck_file`; Java `GeneratedDeck.parse` splits it with limit 4, so the rule never folds into the last card's name). A game is steered when either of its decks carries a rule (`FE/DeckRules.java`; no cap property): every record it writes carries both decks' rules, de-duplicated and sorted, as `aimed_at` in place of the run's `[]` (a directed fork's record keeps its own rule), and its intervention scheduler first forces the lines of the variant cards each player holds, under the same budget and once-per-line sets, before the pass for lines that branch on whose turn it is (FR-058e). A variant is named `<card> Variant <h>`, `h` the first eight hex digits of the SHA-256 of its perturbed script before renaming (`script_variants.variant_digest`), so a file already under that name is the same script: it is reused as it stands and never rewritten, since an earlier run's records join it by key. Every variant, steered ones included, is written with its sidecar before any deck holds it and stays in the tree. **No variant is generated from a held-out card** — perturbing a parameter changes the text, so the variant's text is not itself held out and would reach training, teaching that card's mechanics under an edit the split cannot see. Nor may a variant of any other card print a held-out text, or its template under `--holdout-unit template`: a perturbation can land on one, so once the sidecars exist every variant whose lines print one, under the holdout `build-corpus` chooses, is left out of the decks, and deleted with its sidecar only when this run wrote it (a reused one may have records already); the command takes `build-corpus`'s `--cards-folder` and three holdout flags with the same defaults, and has to be passed the same values. A variant exists on the script surface only and is never converted to prose. A sidecar pass (`VariantSidecarMain`) runs before the first game: a variant without one produces records nothing can join, and the join is designed to fail loudly. It writes the names Forge's card database loaded to `loadable.lst` in the tree (`collect_variants.LOADABLE_FILE`) — never a `.txt`, because the whole tree is staged into Forge's custom-cards directory and Forge reads every `.txt` there as a card script — and only those are decked. When the run ends, however it ends, the staged scripts are taken back out of Forge's custom-cards directory (`infrastructure/variant_staging.py`, by the staging's `.staged-by-effects.list`). The records of the run's shards are counted, and the command exits 1 when there are none: every deck materializing as basic lands — which is what a staged script Forge's card database never registered produces — otherwise plays its matches, writes its progress lines and reports success having collected nothing.

- **`validate-corpus`** — measures the corpus invariants over a shard directory and exits non-zero on any breach: `record_id` uniqueness, one game per `game_id`, link-half pairing, whether trigger/rewrite/resolution records name an acting line and whether an empty one says why, whether every `keyword` provenance key joins the sidecar of the card it names, whether every record was collected in `patched` mode, whether `outcome` and the cost fields carry anything at all, snapshot-tier uniformity across kinds, the per-kind exact-duplicate rate, the duplicate-**event** rate inside a single record, the trigger fired:not-fired ratio per evaluated mode as well as in aggregate, and whether a fork's snapshot agrees with the record it mirrors about the turn. Every invariant reports **the number it measured** whether it passed or not, because a threshold a run barely clears is what an operator needs to see. Meant for the first few minutes of a collection pass, not for a finished corpus.

  Two rules keep the report worth reading. **Judged and watched are separate**: a check with no settled threshold prints `[WATCH]`, reports its number every pass and cannot fail a run — that is how the `zone_change` `from_zone` share, the `attributed_to` breakdown (per resolution record and, separately, per fork record), the `cause` population rate, the non-keyword provenance join and the probe count are carried, and giving one a floor (`--min-attributed-rate`, `--min-zone-change-from-zone-rate`, `--min-fork-attributed-rate`, `--min-cause-rate`) promotes it to a verdict without changing what is measured. A new number is published watched **first**; failing runs on a check nobody has calibrated is the same mistake as an invariant that fails on every good run. And **an invariant that fails on every good run is worse than no invariant**: "each `game_id` names one game" failed 168 of 177 games on a healthy smoke corpus, every one of them the mana-reservoir flush documented in `collection-internals.md` § Caps and sampling, so that case is exempted precisely — a resolution half holding a `mana_produced` and otherwise only the `tapped` events of what its cost tapped, recognised by its payload rather than by the acting card — and so is a fired trigger record, which the per-line trigger reservoir also writes at the game's end with the board of the turn it fired (an unfired one is written as drawn and stays checked); the measurement says how many records the exemption covered. A gen-3 cost half standing alone, its item countered or still on the stack at the game's end, is a paired link: it was written at the cast with its `link_id`, before anyone knew the item's fate.

- **`field-coverage`** — names every record field a corpus never varied (`collection-internals.md` § Why `field-coverage` exists); on a corpus holding gen-3 records it ends with one row per field of the gen-3 snapshot (`field_coverage.GEN3_FIELDS`): varies, constant or absent.
