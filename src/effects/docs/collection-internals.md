# Collection internals

What a record's board and events mean, and the rules the Java collectors keep. Read before changing a collector, a hook, a cap flag or the random seat, and before interpreting what a record's board, events, duration or absence of an event means.

- What is specified: `specs/2026-10-30-ability-effect-model-gen3.md` § 8 (record moments are § 8.14).
- Code: `forge-connector/src/main/java/com/pricepredictor/connector/effects/` (`BusBracketCollector`, `HeldResolutionWindow`, `ManaChannel`, `RewriteChannel`, `StatDiffer`, `StaticSuppression`, `PatchHooks`, `CollectionCaps`).
- Forks: `forks.md`. The record format: `record-format.md`.

## Record moments

Each record's board is read just before the moment it describes, and its events are what the moment changed.

- A spell's cost half reads before its card moves to the stack; an activated ability's before its costs are paid. It is written at the cast event with no `outcome`. A triggered ability or a copy writes none. Its `costs` read the paid lists, the ability's convoke list and the cast card's delve list (`PaidCosts`, `CostChannel`), and the counters and life the payment published (`PaymentLedger`, kept per paying ability and read at the cast event, since neither survives on the ability): `life` is their sum and `counters` a signed net count per type, so a loyalty -2 reads `{LOYALTY: -2}`.
- The effect half reads when its resolution starts (a mode's half at its first clause) and carries `outcome`: `resolved`, `fizzled`, `partially_fizzled` or `declined`. A fizzled or declined spell's half holds exactly its card's move off the stack; an ability's and a spell copy's hold none. `outcome` is `null` on a mana record and the rescue record.
- A spell's effect half takes its card's move off the stack after its own events, before the state-based deaths it holds.
- A countered item writes no effect half. The countering clause's half holds its `spell_countered` and the card's move off the stack.
- A cost half's events are listed but have not happened yet, so no per-entity output reads them.
- What a cast's move onto the stack set off, and what a bracket gathered before its resolution started, precede the effect half's board and are counted as dropped.
- At the game's end every bracket left, on the stack or not, counts what it gathered as dropped, by type and phase, as the orphans are: an item still on the stack had only its cost half written, and its effect half never comes.
- A mana record reads before its mana ability's costs and holds a `tapped` event per permanent its cost tapped, the other events of its own payment (a creature sacrificed for mana), then `mana_produced`, then what its own sub-abilities did (a pain land's damage to its controller). Its `tapped` events read `unresolved`/`instant` and its `mana_produced` `root`/`instant`. Zero mana is no event: a mana ability that produces none (Gaea's Cradle with no creature, whose mana string Forge passes as `0`) writes its record with its `tapped` events and no `mana_produced`, and one with nothing at all to record writes no record. A mana ability whose effect never reports at all (Forge skips the mana hook for an empty mana string, Jeweled Amulet with nothing noted, and when a `ProduceMana` replacement prevents the mana) ends at the resolved signal of the clause that carries its mana part and writes the same record; a mana ability whose mana is a sub-ability's below a non-`Mana` root (Nykthos, Foraging Wickermaw) is not over at its root's signal, which Forge sends before the sub-abilities run, so its record keeps its cap key and the mana. Mana a resolving spell or trigger makes is that half's `mana_produced`, never a mana record.
- A permanent entering the battlefield tapped gets a `tapped` event after its `zone_change`.
- Every library card a snapshot holds renders as it was in the library: untapped, no counters, controlled by its owner, face up.
- A rewrite record reads before the replacement runs.
- `conditions_met` is Forge's own answer, and a clause whose effect did not run files no event.
- On a degraded worker, with no hook, both boards fall back to the one read at the cast event. Where the resolution hook is installed (for a fork, the live game's), a trigger or a copy reads no board at its push (`CastTracker.boardAtPush`): it has no cost half, and its effect half reads the board taken as its resolution starts.

## The hook inventory

- `PatchHooks.REQUIRED` lists every class, method and the record channel it feeds. It is load-bearing, not documentation: `AttributionMode` probes `PatchHooks.SUB_ABILITY`, `ContinuousChannel.CAN_SUPPRESS` reads the six `…Without` entries, and `PatchHooks.report()` prints what is missing at worker startup. The worker's status line carries the same inventory, `PatchHooks.gaps()`, beside the mode, as JSON for the supervisor (`specs/025-ability-effect-model-gen3/contracts/cli.md` § Match worker stop protocol), which stops a collection whose engine is not whole (`collection-runs.md` § The workers' engine).
- `PatchHooks.report()` names, per hook, an absent method and a listener short of what the collectors read: a callback its interface does not declare, or one declared with fewer arguments than `Callback.read` counts (the optional tail included, such as the replacement listener's `ReplacementResult`). A stale installed Forge jar keeps every setter's name, so presence alone reads it whole while a channel stays empty or loses a member. The private `PlayerControllerAi.noteEffectRecordConfirm`, through which a directed fork reports its forced answer, is listed too (`PatchHooks.NOTE_CONFIRM`) and called only through it. A call that still arrives short of a callback's `arity` at run time is counted by `HookFailures.countShortCall`.
- The private Forge members the connector reads are branch accessors, each a `PatchHooks.REQUIRED` hook named by `EngineAccessorHooksTest`, never a forced-access read: `MagicStack.getEffectRecordPrimaryAbility` (`PRIMARY_ABILITY`, `StackActions`), `StaticEffects.getEffectRecordStaticEffects`, `Card.getEffectRecord{ChangedCardTypes,ChangedCardColors,ColorLayers,ChangedCardManaCost,HiddenExtrinsicKeywords,CantHaveKeywords,TempControllers}` and `CardState.getEffectRecord{SpellAbilities,Triggers,StaticAbilities,ReplacementEffects}Through(changes)` (`ForgeFields`). Each hands out the engine's own table or map, never a copy. A stock Forge lacks them, so each read answers null or empty and the snapshot loses that fact (stderr names the absent primary-ability accessor once). The mana cost change record's `mana`/`additional` are not listed, as the colour change record's are not: the branch makes `Card.CardManaCost` public.
- Attribution mode is detected at worker startup, never compiled in. `../forge` is rebuilt independently of this repository, so a build-time switch would mislabel a corpus collected on a checkout without the hooks. Every record carries the detected `mode`.
- With the hooks a run reaches all eight sampling classes. Without them it reaches three, and every record is stamped `degraded`.
- A partly-applied patch is the state that matters. The mode still reads `patched`, because that is one hook's answer, while a channel the run meant to collect is quietly empty. Read the report line, not just the mode.
- The connector builds against stock Forge and reaches every hook by name. A typo in a hook's name therefore shows up at runtime as `degraded`, not at compile time, which is why every record carries the detected mode and `tests/integration/test_effects_patched.py` asserts it.
- The tests read the checkout rather than assuming it. One that pinned `degraded` would fail the day the patch landed, with nothing wrong.
- Forge widens a listener by adding a `void` default method or a same-name overload with appended arguments, and a proxy that does not know the new call answers with nothing and keeps working. `PatchHooksTest` therefore fails on any listener method Forge declares that no collector reads; there is no allow-list. The full table of what each hook hands over is `specs/025-ability-effect-model-gen3/research.md` C1a.
- A listener's callback runs the live game's body and then the fork's, in `try { live } finally { fork }` (`PatchedCollectors.routing`, `PhaseSignals.Sink.both` likewise): a live handler that throws must not hide the callback from the fork playing on. The listener proxy answers `hashCode`, `equals` and `toString` itself (`Listener`), so Forge may keep it in a collection. A null-ability outcome belongs to the game of its `source` or `subjects` objects (`LiveGame.ownsNullAbilityOutcome`); the thread's fork flag decides only an outcome that names no object. The state-based loop's depth is counted once, in `PhaseSignals`, which tells its sinks of the outermost begin and its matching end only.

What the collectors must read of the hooks:

- `onReplacement`'s sixth argument, `buffered`, is the ability a damage replacement set aside. It has not run when the replacement reports and is never `replacedBy`; when it is the sub-ability of a `ReplaceDamage`, `ReplaceSplitDamage` or `ReplaceEffect` ability that ran as `replacedBy` (Forcefield), `ProvenanceResolver` keys both to the root's line, so the record's `replaced_by` and `buffered` are equal. The rewrite record names it in `buffered` and takes the events of its later run as `buffered_events`.
- A null `before` on `onReplacement` means the run parameters could not be copied. The record says `incoming_unknown` and compares nothing.
- An `AddCounter` replacement over several counter types writes one rewrite record per type whose count it changed. A trigger's counter map stays one summed event.
- `onTriggerRun` says a trigger ran. `TriggerChannel` holds a firing only once its evaluation is told run, and one never told writes nothing, so a `trigger` record's `fired` is a trigger Forge ran.
- The collector's own enumeration at a priority stop (`PriorityCandidates.settle`) runs inside `LiveGame.enumerate`, and what it sets off is not the game's. Its rules queries read a candidate on a stand-in card (a morph's face-down cast, a face-down permanent turned up, a spell with an alternate host), and Forge then re-applies every static to the live game and runs its state triggers: every state trigger is evaluated, and one whose condition holds is run and queued for the stack. `TriggerChannel` drops every evaluation and run reported on the enumerating thread, so none reaches a record or the negative sampler, and `QueuedTriggers` takes off the triggers the enumeration queued before the scope ends, by identity, leaving any the game had queued (the list read through the branch's `MagicStack.getSimultaneousStackEntries`, `PatchHooks.SIMULTANEOUS_STACK_ENTRIES`). The flag belongs to one `LiveGame` and one thread: a fork's collectors have their own, and the AI's own queries, on its own thread or for the player with priority, stay visible. The static check runs with no events (`checkStaticAbilities(false)`), so the bus and the stat differ hear nothing, and its zone correction finds nothing at a stop, where the state-based check before priority has made it. A candidate the random seat reads later, outside `settle`, is the seat's own query.
- A forbidden blocker's `responsible_static` comes from `StaticAbilityCantAttackBlock.cantBlockStatic(blocker)` when no static aims at the pair; with neither it is the creature's own can't-block keyword line (`responsible_own`), and an unkeyable static writes `responsible_unresolved`.
- `EffectRecordHook.failureCounts()` is printed by the worker's failure report at every match's end, whatever ended it.
- The state-based-loop signals also bracket the checks made where no player gets priority: the cleanup step's, one for an effect ending the turn, and two for an effect ending the combat phase.
- Forge's `GameEventCardDamaged` fires for a creature only; damage to a planeswalker or battle that is no creature publishes counter events. Its type reads `Deathtouch` whenever the source has deathtouch, even when wither or infect turned the damage into -1/-1 counters.

## Where the events come from

- Forge's bus publishes an event of its own only for outcomes that have one: damage, counters, zone changes, taps. A pump, an anthem recompute, an animation and a colour change all publish only "this card's stats changed".
- `StatDiffer` remembers each card's computed characteristics and describes the difference. That is the source of `pt_change`, `keyword_change` (Forge's hidden keywords too, flagged `hidden`), `ability_change` (abilities gained by line key, or lost; not during an `Animate` clause, whose rule writes its own), `type_change`, `color_change` and `control_change`. A clause that only gains control publishes no stats change, so a battlefield zone event that adds a card reads its `control_change` alone (`StatDiffer.controlChange`, called from `BusEventReader.onZone`). A draw, a discard and a mill are classified from the zone pair. Without these the "gets +2/+2 and gains flying" family resolved into an empty event list.
- A library-to-graveyard move is filed as a `card_milled` provisionally and kept only when Forge's mill flag is set (`MillCheck`; a surveil's bin counts). The flag is set after the move is published and is lost when the card moves on, so a check settles at the first of: the next move out of the same library, the card's removal from its owner's graveyard (`GameEventZone` `Removed`, matched by card id, read off the card object the check kept), or a step boundary. A later clause of the same resolution that returns the card (Grapple with the Past) therefore still counts it as milled.
- A destruction and a sacrifice are told just before the permanent moves and are filed where its move is filed (`destroyed`, `sacrificed`). A face turning is read off the stats change as the face now up (`face_change`).
- The game's outcome (`player_lost`, `player_won`) has a filing of its own (`BusBracketCollector.fileGameOutcome`): the effect half of a `LosesGame` or `WinsGame` ability that ended the game, else the combat record while a damage step's window is open, else the game-end record. It never joins the bracket of a cast or activation still on the stack, which is never written: a "pay life" cost paid to 0 would otherwise lose the game's result.
- The game-end record holds the outcome only, on the board read at the game's end. The held half of a resolution that ended the game is written by `finishGame`, after its spell's card's move off the stack.
- The game-end marker follows only a game whose outcome reached a written live record (`CollectorContext.wroteTheOutcome`, `GameRecording.endedWhole`); one whose outcome was lost reads as cut. So does a game open while a block flush failed: `RecordShardWriter` cuts the file back to its length before the append (`ShardAppender`), keeps the block for the next flush, and marks every open game (`flushFailed`), which `endedWhole` reads. Three appends in a row fail (`MAX_FAILED_APPENDS`) and the writer fails for good: it drops the kept block and throws `ShardUnwritable`, an `Error` naming the shard path and the last `IOException`, which no `catch (Exception)` between the hook and `main` stops, so the worker dies and the supervisor reports a crash; every later write rethrows it.
- A record's stat changes are changes after its own snapshot. The differ re-reads the battlefield whenever a bracket opened at a cast, a mode's half or a damage step takes its snapshot, because Forge can apply a change long before publishing it. A change the re-read swallows reaches no record and is counted as dropped. It does not re-read while an ability resolves.
- An event's `duration` is read from the acting script's `Duration$`. An undeclared one is what Forge applies: instant for an outcome with no lasting state, end of turn for the APIs Forge ends at end of turn by default (Pump, Animate, Debuff, Protection, Effect and their `All` forms, ChangeText, ExchangePower), permanent otherwise. A duration ending when an object changes (host leaves, untaps, unattached, "as long as") reads `permanent`.
- `attributed_to` is the resolving sub-ability's chain index, counted upward from the clause via `getParent()`, never downward from the bracket's root: Forge resolves a copy, and the two chains are not the same objects.

## The effect half and the state-based loop

- A resolution's effect half is held across the state-based loop that follows it and written at the loop's end signal, not when Forge reports the spell resolved. Forge runs state-based actions after that report, so a record written at it lost every death its own effects caused: a burn spell's target appeared damaged and never dead.
- While held, the half takes every battlefield-leaving move between the loop's begin and end signals, every pass included. The check before the resolution found nothing to do, so whatever this one finds the resolution caused.
- The held half takes deaths only, never a stat change. What a static does because of the resolution's last clause (an Equip's +2/+0, a keyword an Equipment grants, a control change) is in the half all the same: the `effect-record-hooks` branch runs the static check that follows the last clause before it publishes "spell resolved" (`MagicStack.resolveStack`), so its changes reach the bracket while it is open, live and in every fork. No clause is resolving during that check, and an event published then carries the `attributed_to` of the last clause of the resolution's line whose own effect ran (`root` for a one-clause Equip, `0` for Brass Squire's Attach sub-ability; `OpenResolution.lastClauseRan`), and reads its `duration` and `cause` off that clause too (Brass Squire's +2/+0 is `permanent`, the Attach's, not the Pump root's `end_of_turn`), as a change a check inside a clause or between two clauses applies (an Aura spell's move onto the battlefield) carries the clause Forge makes it under; one before any clause ran carries `unresolved`. On stock Forge the check runs after the report and those changes are counted as dropped.
- A trigger reaching the stack between two passes opens its own bracket without closing the held half. A dies trigger's records therefore hold none of the check's deaths; a death its own resolution later causes is the trigger's.
- Every item Forge pushes as it finishes a resolution (two copies of it, a copy and a trigger) is that resolution's consequence, not a player's next action: the finishing window (`StackBrackets.finishing`) stays open from the "spell resolved" report until priority, the next resolution start or the resolved item's own id being cast again, so the held half keeps its deaths however many items are pushed.
- Moves after the loop stay out, so a Treasure the spell made and later sacrificed for the next spell does not join it.
- A state-based death with no held half and no open damage step reaches no record. Example: a creature a -1/-1 counter cost leaves at 0 toughness.
- On a modal resolution a death goes to the mode half whose events named the permanent, otherwise to the last half.
- A combat damage step's record holds its own check: the loop's end closes it, not a trigger put on the stack inside the loop.
- On a worker without the loop hook the half falls back to closing at the next action, and a move counts as the check's while the tracker is frozen.
- A forced resolution runs the check itself, with no loop signals: its held half closes at the next action, or when the fork's recording closes (`ForcedResolution`).

## Dropped events

- Every event that reaches no record is counted per game by event type and engine phase (`FE/DroppedEvents.java`): one at a player's action at priority that is no cast, one a cost payment publishes, an orphan (filed while no bracket was open) discarded at the next cast, resolution end or game end, a state-based death nothing took.
- A protection or directed fork counts its own; a kept one's are added to the live game's under the fork's kind (`forks.md` § Directed forks).
- The counts are written per game, not at exit, because workers are killed with their process tree and run no shutdown hook. File format: `record-format.md`.

## Continuous records

- A `continuous` record's snapshot is the board without its own static. The label is what the static does, so a snapshot carrying the static's work would hand the model the answer with the question.
- The board is recomputed with the static's layer entries dropped, not stripped of what it contributed. A creature printed with trample keeps trample when the anthem granting it is removed; subtracting the contribution would describe a board that never existed. Expect a few percent of contributions to survive their own snapshot for that reason.
- Every channel of the entity reads that board through one object, `StaticSuppression`, and no static lists what it affects.
- A player's `untapped_production` reads that board too (`UntappedProduction`): the mana abilities and controllers without the static, no production from a creature whose haste is the static's, and a {T} host sick only as the static's creature (an animated land played this turn) read as not sick. Forge's activation checks read the live board only (an activator must be the card's live controller; "permanents you control" reads live controllers; metalcraft counts live types; "each colour among permanents you control" reads live colours), so the read is made on the board `LentBoard` lends, every card its type line, colours and controller without the static as for a static line's `active` below, and a host's sickness is lifted, each put back in a `finally`; a read that left any of them on the live card would change the game it describes. Mox Opal's metalcraft that only Mycosynth Lattice's types meet makes nothing on the Lattice's record, and Bloom Tender makes no blue on Painter's Servant's. `max_land_plays` needs no suppression: an `AdjustLandPlays$` static affects players only, contributes to no card and writes no continuous record.
- A permanent the static took control of is as summoning sick on that board as it was for the player who controls it there (`StaticSuppression.sick`), so a creature that player had controlled since their turn began is not sick on the static's record of the turn it was taken (302.6). Forge keeps one sickness flag, which the zone correction of a control change (`GameAction.controllerChangeZoneCorrection`) sets for the new controller; the patch keeps the flag for the old one at the change (`Card.isEffectRecordSickForPriorController`, false once a turn of that player's has begun since), read when the card sits on another player's battlefield than its controller's on that board. Without the hook, or for a controller there who is not the one the card had before its most recent control change, the live flag is read.
- A static line's `active` reads that board too (`LineInForce`, through `LentBoard`): its condition on the board without the static, so metalcraft that only Mycosynth Lattice's types meet reads `false`. Forge's `checkConditions()` reads the live board only, so for the read of a static whose condition can read a type line, colours or controller (`LentBoard.conditionReadsTheBoard`) every live card is lent its type line without the static (the type line Forge caches for the card's state, `CardState.lendEffectRecordType`; the type layer tables are not touched), its colours without it (Forge caches no colour, so the static's cells are set aside from the colour tables every colour read computes from and the same cells put back, `Card.setAsideEffectRecordColors`; the tables are sorted by their keys, so putting back restores them exactly) and every permanent its controller without it (`StaticSuppression.lendController`), each put back in a `finally`. The two lends are branch methods reached through `PatchHooks.LEND_TYPE` and `PatchHooks.SET_ASIDE_COLORS`, never reflective writes: a checkout without them is named by `PatchHooks.report()`, and its records read those conditions on the live board. Power and toughness, keywords and abilities are not lent, nor the battlefield zone Forge moved a stolen permanent into, so a condition on those (ferocious; metalcraft counting an artifact Control Magic took) reads them live.
- A contribution carries `set_pt` only when its static sets a base and `controller` only when it gives control, so a static whose only change is a set base (Godhead of Awe) or control (Control Magic) is a record of its own.
- A checkout whose patch predates the `…Without` methods collects no continuous records at all rather than leaking ones.
- Continuous records are read at every phase boundary but the untap step's. No player gets priority there, and the live game has not yet re-applied its statics for the new turn, so a record there could describe a board the rules do not hold.
- `types` and `colors` are each one flat token list: a bare token is added, `-x` removed, and a lone `=` marks a line the static sets outright.
- Subtypes and supertypes are recorded and then dropped at target derivation, because `types_gained` and `types_lost` cover the core types only. The record is the archive; a head reads a subset.
- Under a `=` the lost fields are the entity's own types and colours on the board without the static, less what the `=` sets; an entity not on that board leaves them unknown (`effect_targets.py`).

## Token script ids

- `token_script_id` changed value space on 2026-09-17. Before, it was the token's printed name; after, the token's script stem (`c_a_food_sac`), falling back to the printed name only when the script cannot be named. A reader spanning shards from both sides sees both value spaces in one field.

## Caps and sampling

- The three collectors that repeat themselves (continuous effects, the AI's blocker queries, mana) are capped by identity, not by count. The repeats describe a board that did not move. A count cap would keep the first N and then nothing, concentrating a card's records in whichever games came first.
- `--mana-cap` is keyed on the mana produced as well as the ability, so a dual land records each colour it makes.
- Mana is reservoir-sampled, not taken first. A land's first tap is turn one against an empty board, so keeping it would make every mana record describe the same early game.
- Mana records are therefore held until `close()`, and a JVM that dies loses that game's reservoir. The state snapshot is taken when the mana was made, not at flush. A triggered mana ability whose payment Forge reports (Wild Growth's) is a mana record of its own, keyed to its own line.
- A game's end-of-game records pass `--max-records-per-game`, each bounded on its own: fired trigger records (24 per trigger line), mana records (`--mana-cap` per mana key), the rewrite records still waiting on a buffered ability at the game's end, the live record holding the outcome (never a fork's: `forks.md` § Rules for every fork), and the game-end marker.
- Every random consumer of a game draws from its own stream, derived from the game's seed and a fixed `SeedStreams.Consumer` label (seat draw, sampler, intervention choices, spread gate, directed-fork draws, protection draws, play-forward seeds, fork streams): a `SplittableRandom` rooted at the seed xor the label's salt, so no two consumers of one game replay each other's draws (a match's first game is seeded with its start time XORed with 40 bits hashed from the worker's `{worker}-{lifetime}` shard token, `SeedStreams.matchSeed`, so two workers whose matches start in the same millisecond draw different streams; the seed also seeds the seat draw, and the match's later games add their index), and a new consumer shifts no other's stream. `SplittableRandom` mixes its state, which matters because games are seeded consecutively and a linear congruential generator's first output is nearly a linear function of its seed. Never seed a consumer from the bare game seed or reuse a salt.
- The eight cap flags are per-worker-process quantities the Python supervisor cannot observe. They reach the JVM as `-Deffect.*` system properties, and `CollectionCaps.fromSystemProperties()` falls back to a default rather than failing.
- The cap values, defaults and `-D` property names are defined once, in `CollectionCaps` (`sealed/domain/collection_caps.py`, re-exported by `effects.domain.collection_caps`), and `sealed match-outcomes` builds its property dict from it (`worker_cap_properties` in `sealed.infrastructure.effect_collection_flags`, which also holds the flags both CLIs import and `positive_int`/`non_negative_int`; `sealed` never imports `effects`). `run.json`'s resolved caps are `caps_manifest` over the same class. `tests/unit/effects/domain/test_collection_caps.py` pins `CollectionCaps` against the property literals in `CollectionCaps.java`. A property one side reads and the other never writes is invisible from both.
- `--probe-keywords` is spelled as the JVM compares it (lower case, spaces as `_`), and a keyword gate 2 does not check is refused at parse time.
- An empty `--probe-keywords` takes no probe fork at all, whatever `--probes-per-game` says. Every collecting command prints its probe state before the first worker spawns, and `validate-corpus` reports the probe count as a watched number. The JVM's own fallback, with no property set, is the same eight keywords (`CollectionCaps.DEFAULT_PROBE_KEYWORDS`, pinned against the Python default by `test_collection_caps.py`); the property set blank probes nothing.
- Snapshot depth is not a flag. `SnapshotBuilder` renders all four inclusion tiers into every record. A depth that varied by collector made `state.tiers` a proxy for how a record was collected, which the schema bars from the model. The field is still written because older shards carry shorter vectors; readers treat an absent tier as uncollected, not empty.

## Random seat

- Under `--random-seat-share F --random-seat-probability P`, one seat in a share `F` of matches is a `RandomSeatController`, a `PlayerControllerAi` subclass. Forge itself is unchanged.
- It exists because the AI never casts some cards at all. A random seat casts them for real, where an intervention forks past their cost and choices.
- At every priority, attack, block and target decision it draws uniformly from the legal options with probability `P`. It never passes while a non-mana ability is playable, and it leaves the land drop, mana payment and mulligans to the Forge AI.
- The seat's sacrifice, discard, exile and tap choices are drawn by `RandomSpellPlayer.RandomCostDecision` inside `CostPayment.payComputerCosts`, after the card has moved to the stack. The engine draws every part's decision on the board as it stands, before it pays any part, and pays mana first, so a draw never fails for lack of a source the mana payment used: the play is made or fails before any payment, and `RandomSeatCostChoiceTest` pins that the card is never left in the stack zone. A tap choice that names a creature the mana payment tapped is paid by tapping it again, which the engine does not refuse.
- What a resolving effect asks it is the Forge AI's, but for a mandatory single choice the AI answers with nothing although options are offered: the seat draws that one uniformly among the options from its own stream (`RandomSeatController.optionIndex`, a `MandatoryChoiceAi`). Forge's `AttachAi` declines an attachment it finds useless whether or not the choice is optional, so a drawn Equip onto a creature that cannot attack or block resolved doing nothing.
- What a clause may target is enumerated once, by `TargetingClauses.candidatesOf`, for the draw, the verdict's `has_legal_target`, a decision record's `legal_targets` and a charm's choosable modes (`TargetingClauses.chainReachesMinimum`; the random seat's mode draw counts them inside `TargetingClauses.withNoneChosenOnModes`, so targets the AI's evaluation left on a mode never narrow a cross-target rule). A clause whose target zone is the stack alone (Counter, CopySpellAbility, ChangeTargets, ControlSpell, `ChangeZone | Origin$ Stack`) is offered the stack items it can target as the spell ability itself, never its card: those effects read spell targets only, so a card drawn there resolves doing nothing and the spell it meant to answer resolves after it. `legal_targets` lists such an item as its card's ref.
- The verdict, `legal_targets` and the sample count with no target chosen: `PlayabilityVerdict.asActivator` runs them inside `TargetingClauses.withNoneChosen`, which sets aside the targets on every clause the candidate offers and puts the same objects back after, also on a throw. Forge's `canTarget` judges a cross-target rule (unique targets, different controllers, a shared type, a total mana value) against the clause's and its parents' chosen targets, and the AI leaves its picks on a spell it did not cast, so counted without that those picks would be the only legal targets. The draw is never wrapped: it fills one clause after another and needs the earlier clauses' picks.
- It does not draw again, until the turn ends, a free activation that changed nothing (`FruitlessActivations`), nor one still on the stack. Free means it paid nothing the game keeps (`PaidCosts.paidNothingKept`): no mana, no card moved (sacrificed, discarded, exiled, returned, delved), no life and no counter moved, so a loyalty ability or a pay-life ability is never free. A tap, an untap symbol, a reveal, an exert and a convoke tap do not make an activation costly. An activation no printed line made (one an Aura or Equipment grants) is remembered by its card and ability text. The entries of abilities no longer on the stack are dropped at each turn change, in every game.
- It never draws a play whose life payment (`CostPayLife`, summed) would leave it at 0 or less (`LifeCostGate`); the verdict still calls such a play playable. Nor does it draw an attach line (Equip) whose only legal target is the creature its host is already attached to (`RepeatAttachGate`): 701.3b makes it do nothing, and the seat never passes while one is playable. A priority stop whose enumeration throws leaves no stop for the seat to take: `PriorityCandidates.settle` clears the last one first.
- Its matches write effect records only, never a match-outcome or cards-played row.
- A share above 0 without a probability is refused.
- Every real decision is written and no what-if is. The AI re-asks one combat many times while planning, and a query drawn before de-duplication survives in proportion to how often it was asked. Blocks against attacks the AI rejected reach the corpus instead as real decisions against the random seat.

## State kept per game

- A registry the collectors key by game (`LibraryCapture`'s built library cards, `ClauseNotes`' clause notes) drops a game's entry when its `GameRecording` closes, after the game-end marker, and a fork's when `Forks.ForkRun.take` ends or, for a copy discarded before it is handed on (its score check failed), when `Forks.copyAndVerify` returns (`Forks.release`): the copy's fidelity audit has already snapshotted it. The maps are weak-keyed, but a built card, and an open note's clause, hold their game: an entry never released keeps its game, every card in it and every fork of it in the worker's heap, until some thirty games into a JVM's life its games slow tenfold and it dies of `OutOfMemoryError` (`GameRetentionTest`).

## Dead fields

- A collector that writes `"blocking":[]` produces records byte-identical to ones for a creature that genuinely is not blocking. The shape is right, the contract tests pass, and the field is dead. `field-coverage` exists to name every field a corpus never varied.
- `KNOWN_CONSTANT_FIELDS` (`application/field_coverage.py`) lists the fields known dead, and is empty. The integration test fails when a listed field starts carrying data, so the list can only shrink. It is asserted in that direction only, because a field that is merely rare looks exactly like one that is unwired.
