# August 5, 2026 — Speccing the gen-3 online trainer

**TL;DR:** Closed out the gen-2 RL post-mortem and turned the gen-3 online
critic-free GRPO design into an implementable spec through the full speckit
pipeline. Caught two real design mistakes while writing the spec text itself
(wasteful Forge restarts, drafts piloted by the wrong policy), plus another
during the final consistency pass.

I finished writing up the gen-2 verdict: across two separate yardstick
corpora, both a lightly-collapsed and a maximally-collapsed checkpoint
landed at tie-to-minus-one-sigma against Forge on argmax deck quality. So
the offline RL loss diving to -3 during training turned out to be cosmetic
to the actual objective — the damage stayed in the probability tail that
argmax deck-building never reads. That went into the gen-2 experiment doc,
alongside the gen-3 online GRPO design doc: single-term loss, no critic, no
KL anchor, no entropy bonus, and the anchor-margin metric as a live
progress signal, since a real yardstick run can't happen without pausing
training.

From there I moved into speccing the actual "online trainer" feature
through speckit: specify, clarify, plan, tasks, analyze. Two corrections
came up while reviewing the drafted spec text. First, Forge pays roughly 20
seconds of JVM startup per process, so a design that reused
generate-draft-data unchanged on every round would spend most of its time
restarting Forge instead of drafting — the fix was a single resident Forge
worker held for the whole run rather than one per round. Second, and more
consequential, the draft spec said the ten fresh drafts each round were
generated by the frozen gen-1 anchor, when the actual intent was for the
learner seats to be piloted by the live, currently-training policy every
round — I pointed out that otherwise the design wasn't really solving
gen-2's problem at all. I also noted a single Forge worker is enough
regardless, since draft generation itself doesn't play any games; only
game-play is the slow part of Forge.

I also cleaned up the CLI surface for the new command: too many things
were generically named "agent" or "checkpoint", so I had it reorganized
around three agent categories, each with its own flag prefix — Forge-
piloted (no binding needed), frozen (a label bound to a checkpoint), and
exactly one learner (label plus warm-start checkpoint together). I added a
requirement that a draft needs at least one learner seat to be worth
generating at all, and caught that the scorer checkpoint was missing from
the CLI even though it's what turns a built deck into the reward signal in
the first place.

I had the whole document workflow written into CLAUDE.md so I wouldn't
have to keep re-explaining it: experiments/ for rationale and benchmark
results, root-level dated specs for a human-readable normative WHAT/HOW,
speckit's spec.md as the implementation-facing artifact, and the four-step
loop of experiment doc, spec, speckit implementation, run-and-analyze,
repeat.

The plan and tasks passes surfaced more before any code got written.
Planning flagged that guaranteeing at least one learner seat per pod needs
a small Java change in DraftWorkerMain, and that the project's own
duplication rule forces extracting several already-duplicated training
helpers into shared modules touching four existing trainers. The analyze
pass then caught that the "at least one learner seat" requirement was
never actually wired to the launch site in the generated tasks — a silent
failure that would have let learner-free pods get drafted, built, scored,
and trained on with no error — and that the plan's claimed single shared
card locator was never actually passed through anywhere, so cards would
have been decompressed twice per lookup instead of once. I decided to drop
the --seed flag entirely once it became clear sampling had moved into the
Forge workers themselves, so a single top-level seed would either do
nothing or make every worker draw the identical draft sequence.
